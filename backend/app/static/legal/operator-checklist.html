Operator Checklist

For the operator of Coach Studio — not shown to end users.

This page is a private reminder list, not legal advice and not a public policy. It is set to noindex and is not linked from the app footer. Work through it (with a qualified lawyer and, for insurance, a broker) before offering the Service to the public. Delete or hide this page before a real launch if you prefer.

1. Business registration & insurance

These protect you personally more than any document on the site does.

  • Form a business entity (e.g. an LLC, or the local equivalent). It separates your personal assets from the app's liabilities, so a claim against Coach Studio does not automatically reach your house or savings.
  • Get insurance. General liability and professional liability (errors & omissions) coverage pays for what the disclaimers in the Terms cannot fully waive. If the coaching activity itself carries injury risk, ask a broker about that specifically.
  • Once formed, replace the [your name / business name], [country / state], and [your jurisdiction] placeholders across the Privacy Policy and Terms with the entity's real details.

2. Cookie / consent banner (only if you add analytics)

Today the app sets a single essential session cookie, so no banner is required. The moment you add advertising, tracking, or third-party analytics cookies, you must show EU/UK visitors a consent banner and only set those cookies after consent. The Privacy Policy already commits you to this — build the banner before you add the tracking, not after.

3. Payment terms (only if you start charging)

The Terms include a Fees & payment section covering billing, auto-renewal, and refunds. Before taking money: pick a payment processor (so you never store card numbers yourself), confirm the auto-renewal disclosures required in your market, and align the Terms' refund language with what you actually offer.

4. Real deletion mechanism

The Privacy Policy promises users (and parents/guardians of players) that they can request deletion of their data. Right now that is a manual process over email. Before you have many users, either (a) commit to honoring email requests promptly and keep a record, or (b) build a self-service Delete my account & data button in-app. The button is the more defensible option and removes the manual bottleneck.

5. Breach-notification plan

The Privacy Policy promises breach notification. Have a concrete plan ready before you need it: how you would detect a breach, who you would contact, a template notice, and the deadline that applies to you (often within 72 hours of becoming aware, e.g. under GDPR). Know in advance which authority you would report to in your jurisdiction.

6. Data-processing agreements (DPAs)

Where the law requires it, put a DPA in place with each provider that processes personal data on your behalf — at minimum your AI provider (Anthropic) and your hosting/database provider. Check each provider's site for a standard DPA you can accept, and keep a copy on file.

7. Minors' data — verifiable consent

This is your largest exposure. Because coaches may enter data about children, consider going beyond the policy language:

  • Prefer not storing directly identifying information about minors unless you have verifiable parental consent (COPPA) or a clear lawful basis (GDPR/UK GDPR).
  • Encourage or enforce data minimization for players (first names / initials rather than full details).
  • Provide a straightforward route for a parent or guardian to request deletion of a child's data (ties into item 4).
  • Get a lawyer's review specifically on the child-data question before operating at any scale.

8. Before launch — fill every placeholder

Search the legal pages for the highlighted placeholders and replace them all: effective date, business name, jurisdiction/governing law, contact email, liability cap, hosting provider, and response/retention timeframes. Then have the whole set reviewed by a qualified lawyer for your jurisdiction.
